Cayley Graphs

Theory, Classifications for Orders 4, 6, 8, and 10, and Gallery

Definition: Cayley Graph
Let $G$ be a finite group and $S \subseteq G \setminus \{e\}$ a connection set satisfying $S = S^{-1}$ (symmetric). The Cayley graph $\mathrm{Cay}(G, S)$ is a simple undirected graph defined as:
  • Vertex Set: $V = G$
  • Edge Set: $E = \{\{g, h\} \mid gh^{-1} \in S\} = \{\{g, sg\} \mid g \in G, s \in S\}$
Key Properties:
  • $\mathrm{Cay}(G, S)$ is $|S|$-regular.
  • $\mathrm{Cay}(G, S)$ is connected if and only if $S$ generates the group $G$ ($\langle S \rangle = G$).
  • $\mathrm{Cay}(G, S)$ is always vertex-transitive (the group $G$ acts transitively on itself via left multiplication).
Examples and Non-Examples of Cayley Graphs

Examples:

  • Cycle Graphs $C_n$: $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_n, \{1, n-1\})$ is the $n$-cycle.
  • Hypercubes $Q_d$: $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_2^d, S)$ where $S$ is the standard basis is the $d$-dimensional hypercube.
  • Complete Graphs $K_n$: $\mathrm{Cay}(G, G \setminus \{e\})$ for any group $G$ of order $n$.
  • Prism Graphs: $\mathrm{Cay}(D_n, \{r, r^{-1}, s\})$ yields the $n$-gonal prism.

Non-Examples:

  • Paths $P_n$ ($n \ge 3$) and Stars $K_{1, n}$: Not vertex-transitive (vertices have different degrees or non-symmetric structural positions), so they can never be Cayley graphs.
  • The Petersen Graph (10 vertices): It is $3$-regular and vertex-transitive, but it is NOT a Cayley graph. No group of order 10 ($\mathbb{Z}_{10}$ or $D_5$) has a connection set that yields the Petersen graph (it lacks the required automorphism symmetries).

Worked Example: Verifying the Pentagonal Prism
Consider $S = \{r, r^4, s\} \subseteq D_5$. Every vertex $g \in D_5$ has neighbors $gr, gr^4, gs$:
  • If $g = r^k$ (rotation), $gr$ and $gr^4$ are rotations, forming an outer 5-cycle. $gs = r^k s$ is a reflection, connecting $g$ to the inner layer.
  • If $g = sr^k$ (reflection), $gr$ and $gr^4$ are reflections, forming an inner 5-cycle. $gs = sr^k s = r^{-k}$ is a rotation, connecting back to the outer layer.
Thus, the resulting Cayley graph consists of two 5-cycles linked by a perfect matching: the Pentagonal Prism.
Important Summary Observations
  • Vertex Transitivity: Every Cayley graph is vertex-transitive. Thus, graphs with asymmetric degree distributions or central vertices (like stars $K_{1, n}$) are never Cayley graphs.
  • Automorphism Orbits: Two connection sets $S_1, S_2 \subseteq G$ produce isomorphic Cayley graphs if there is an automorphism $\phi \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ such that $\phi(S_1) = S_2$.
  • Connectedness Condition: A Cayley graph $\mathrm{Cay}(G, S)$ is connected if and only if $S$ generates $G$.
